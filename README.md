# Public Informatics — Critical Review Papers

> Two critical methodological reviews from the Theory & Practice of Public Informatics course (Rutgers MPI, Prof. Vonu Thakuriah, Fall 2024). The course's empirical module paper — an ARIMA/VAR study of oil shocks and US inflation — lives in its own repo: [oil-shocks-us-inflation-forecasting](https://github.com/umerfarooq7424/oil-shocks-us-inflation-forecasting).

## Module 2 — Hybrid Machine Learning in Housing Price Prediction (Nov 2024)

A critical review of three approaches to predicting house prices:

1. **Hybrid ML on the "Housing Price in Beijing" dataset** — stacked generalization and regression ensembles; the review focuses on feature engineering and ensemble design
2. **Bayesian Optimization + stacking/bagging + Transformer framework (Hong Kong)** — 27 models compared on 13 metrics; the review examines the evaluation strategy and why hybrids won
3. **A structural equilibrium model of the Great Recession housing boom and bust** — house prices, consumption, and credit, where belief shifts mattered more than credit conditions

The review compares the methodologies head-to-head: variables, equations, evaluation strategies, and what each paradigm can and cannot see.

## Module 3 — AI Governance in the Public Sector (Dec 2024)

A critical review of the AI-governance literature, centred on:

- **Kuziemski & Misuraca** — AI deployment case studies in Canada, Poland, and Finland: legal frameworks, implementation challenges, and the efficiency-vs-accountability tension in automated decision-making
- **Zuiderwijk, Chen & Salem** — the transformative potential and risks of AI in public governance

Focus: power asymmetries created by public-sector AI, and the case for a unified framework to evaluate AI's impact on public administration — transparency, fairness, and democratic accountability alongside efficiency.
